\chapter{Data}
\label{ch:data}

This chapter describes the dataset, the quality checks run on its images and masks, and the
data split used in the experiments. The split needs some space because the official TN3K split
turned out to be unsuitable for conformal calibration (Section~\ref{sec:data-official}), which
led to the pooled split used in the later experiments (Section~\ref{sec:data-pooled}).

\section{The TN3K dataset}
\label{sec:data-tn3k}

TN3K is a public thyroid ultrasound dataset released with the TRFE-Net segmentation
model~\citep{gong2021tn3k,gong2023trfe}. It contains 3,493 two-dimensional ultrasound images
from 2,421 patients. Each image comes with a pixel-level segmentation mask of the nodule and a
single benign or malignant label. The images were acquired with different devices and from
different views.

The labels are taken from the cytological biopsy
of the nodule~\citep{gong2022acl}, usually obtained by fine-needle aspiration (FNA), which is the
clinical reference standard for thyroid nodules. The label therefore records the result of the
biopsy, not a radiologist's reading of the image. The same authors note that the biopsy result
does not always agree with the risk suggested by the ultrasound appearance under
TI-RADS~\citep{gong2022acl,tessler2017}.

The dataset comes with an official split into a set for training and validation (\emph{trainval})
and a test set, and images from the same patient appear in only one of the two
(Table~\ref{tab:data-official}).

\begin{table}[ht]
  \centering
  \caption{Official TN3K split.}
  \label{tab:data-official}
  \begin{tabular}{lrrrr}
    \toprule
    official subset & images & benign & malignant & malignant share \\
    \midrule
    trainval & 2,879 & 1,905 & 974 & 33.8\% \\
    test     &   614 &   378 & 236 & 38.4\% \\
    \midrule
    total    & 3,493 & 2,283 & 1,210 & 34.6\% \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Two properties of the released files matter for the rest of this work:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{No physical scale.} The images carry no pixel spacing, so sizes can only be
        measured in pixels or relative to the image, not in millimetres.
  \item \textbf{No patient identifiers.} The patient of each image is not released. Images can
        therefore not be grouped by patient when the data are split again
        (Section~\ref{sec:data-patients}).
\end{itemize}

The masks are stored as JPEG files. Because of compression they contain between 16 and 29
distinct grey values (median 22) instead of two, and they are binarised with a threshold of 127.

\section{Image and mask quality control}
\label{sec:data-qc}

Before feature extraction, every image and mask was checked. All 3,493 images load correctly
and each mask has the same size as its image. Three findings affect the features
(Table~\ref{tab:data-qc}).

\begin{table}[ht]
  \centering
  \caption{Image and mask checks by official subset (medians or shares of images).}
  \label{tab:data-qc}
  \begin{tabular}{lrr}
    \toprule
    & trainval & official test \\
    \midrule
    image width (pixels)               & 390    & 436 \\
    image height (pixels)              & 336    & 375 \\
    nodule area (share of the image)   & 8.6\%  & 7.0\% \\
    masks with more than one component & 7.8\%  & 11.7\% \\
    masks touching the image border    & 11.2\% & 5.4\% \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Image size varies, and official-test images are larger.} Size measured in pixels
        therefore partly measures the image: the major axis length of the nodule correlates with
        image width at $r = 0.42$. Image width alone predicts malignancy weakly (AUC 0.57), which
        points to an acquisition-related confounder. Relative to the image, official-test
        nodules are slightly \emph{smaller}.
  \item \textbf{Multi-component masks.} 298 masks consist of more than one region. These images
        are less often malignant (21.8\% against 35.8\%).
  \item \textbf{Border-touching masks.} 356 masks touch the image border, so part of the nodule
        may lie outside the image. These images are also less often malignant (21.9\% against
        36.1\%).
\end{enumerate}

How these findings were handled in feature extraction is described in
Section~\ref{sec:methods-extraction}.

\section{The official split is not exchangeable}
\label{sec:data-official}

The conformal methods used in this work (Section~\ref{sec:methods-triage}) assume that the
calibration nodules and the nodules the rule is later applied to are
\emph{exchangeable}: informally, that they are drawn from the same distribution. The first
experiments kept the official test set for reporting and split trainval at random, stratified by
label, into train (2,015 images), validation (432) and calibration (432).

\paragraph{Classifier two-sample test.} Exchangeability was checked with a classifier two-sample
test. A logistic regression is trained to tell two samples apart from their standardised
features. Its 5-fold cross-validated AUC is close to 0.5 when the samples come from the same
distribution. A permutation p-value is computed from 500 random relabellings of the two samples,
so the smallest possible value is $1/501 \approx 0.002$.

\begin{table}[ht]
  \centering
  \caption{Two-sample test on the official split, using the eight features selected in the
           first experiments.}
  \label{tab:data-twosample-official}
  \begin{tabular}{lrr}
    \toprule
    comparison & AUC & p-value \\
    \midrule
    validation vs calibration (control) & 0.506 & 0.37 \\
    calibration vs official test        & 0.632 & 0.002 \\
    \quad benign nodules only           & 0.644 & 0.002 \\
    \quad malignant nodules only        & 0.633 & 0.002 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

Calibration and official test are clearly distinguishable
(Table~\ref{tab:data-twosample-official}), and the difference holds within each class, so it is
not explained by the different malignant share. The validation--calibration control is
consistent with exchangeability, as expected for two random parts of the same set.

\paragraph{Not only image size.} Image size explains part of the difference: image width alone
separates official-test images from trainval images with AUC 0.77. The revised features
(Section~\ref{sec:methods-extraction}) express shape relative to the image. If image size were
the only difference, the two sources should become harder to separate with these features. They
do not (Table~\ref{tab:data-sources}): the sources differ in ways that size normalisation does
not remove. The cause is not known; different devices or acquisition periods are possible
explanations.

\begin{table}[ht]
  \centering
  \caption{Two-sample test, trainval (2,879 images) vs official test (614 images). All p-values
           are 0.002.}
  \label{tab:data-sources}
  \begin{tabular}{lr}
    \toprule
    features & AUC \\
    \midrule
    v1 shape features (pixels)   & 0.654 \\
    v2 shape features (relative) & 0.709 \\
    v1, all 67 features          & 0.708 \\
    v2, all 102 features         & 0.805 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

As a consequence, error rates measured on the official test set describe the behaviour of a
calibrated rule under distribution shift; they do not test its guarantee.

\section{The pooled split}
\label{sec:data-pooled}

To obtain calibration and test sets from the same distribution, all 3,493 images were pooled and
split again at random, stratified by label and by official source
(\texttt{experiments/make\_pooled\_splits.py}, seed 42). The proportions are 60\% train, 12.5\%
validation, 15\% calibration and 12.5\% test (Table~\ref{tab:data-pooled}). The split is stored
in \texttt{splits/pooled\_seed42.csv} and is used in all experiments from 8 October 2026 onwards.

\begin{table}[ht]
  \centering
  \small
  \caption{Pooled split.}
  \label{tab:data-pooled}
  \begin{tabular}{lrrr>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{4.2cm}}
    \toprule
    split & images & malignant & from official test & used for \\
    \midrule
    train       & 2,095 & 34.6\% & 17.6\% & fitting, scaling, feature selection \\
    validation  &   436 & 34.6\% & 17.4\% & checkpoint and setting selection \\
    calibration &   524 & 34.7\% & 17.7\% & conformal thresholds only \\
    test        &   438 & 34.7\% & 17.6\% & reporting only \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

With the same two-sample test, validation and calibration (AUC 0.476) and calibration and test
(AUC 0.434, p = 1.0) can no longer be told apart. The guarantees calibrated on this split
therefore apply to its test set. The price is that the test set is no longer an independent
external sample: it estimates performance on the TN3K population as a whole, a mixture of both
official sources.

\subsection{Patient-level independence}
\label{sec:data-patients}

The official split keeps each patient in a single subset. Because patient identifiers are not
released, the pooled split was drawn at image level. Images of the same patient, possibly showing
the same nodule in different views, can therefore appear in both the training and the test
split. The same holds for the train, validation and calibration sets of the first experiments,
which were drawn at image level from trainval.

The share of affected test images is unknown. TN3K has 1,072 more images than patients; under
the most unfavourable distribution of these repeated images, about 40\% of the test images could
have an image of the same patient in the training set. This overlap makes the absolute test
metrics optimistic for previously unseen patients. All models compared on the same split are
exposed to it, so relative comparisons are less affected, although models that use more
features may benefit more from it. The main negative result of this work is not weakened:
overlap can only make the automatic benign group look cleaner, and Learn-then-Test still
certified no automatic decision (Chapter~\ref{ch:results}).
