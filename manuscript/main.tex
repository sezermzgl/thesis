\begin{abstract}
Machine-learning models for thyroid ultrasound are rarely accompanied by a guarantee on their
errors. This thesis studies a triage layer that decides automatically only where the error rate can
be certified: at most 5\% cancers among nodules called benign and at most 20\% benign nodules among
those called malignant, with all other nodules referred to a clinician. The thresholds are set with
Learn then Test, a conformal risk-control method. The underlying classifier follows Ra et al.\
(2025): radiomics features of the nodule are written as text and read by a language model
fine-tuned with QLoRA.

On the TN3K dataset (3,493 images with biopsy-based labels), an audit showed that the official
calibration and test sources are not exchangeable and that the original features had defects; a
pooled split and a revised extraction address both. With the revised features, the test AUC of
Qwen2.5-1.5B rises from 0.70 to 0.75, while XGBoost on the same features reaches 0.81. Stating
values as z-scores changes nothing, replacing them by percentiles hurts, and neither Qwen2.5-7B nor Llama-2-7B with the settings of Ra et al.\ has
helped so far. No automatic decision could be certified in any setting: the model's errors are
stable across perturbed queries, agreement between queries is no better than the softmax
probability, and its cleanest low-risk groups are too small for the evidence required. A weaker
guarantee on the share of missed cancers can be certified, but varies across runs. The thesis
reports work in progress; ongoing experiments are listed at the end.
\end{abstract}
